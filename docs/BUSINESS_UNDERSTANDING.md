## What is Caprae's mission?

Caprae Capital exists to make acquisition entrepreneurship work: for the searchers and operators who buy small and
mid-sized businesses, and for the companies they buy. Unlike most private equity firms, which lean on financial
engineering, Caprae's thesis is that the real value in M&A is created *after* the deal closes, through operational
change. The handbook frames M&A as "a seven-year journey", where the closing is the starting line, not the finish.

To deliver that, Caprae runs as a platform rather than a classic fund. It offers end-to-end private equity services,
from deal sourcing and owner outreach to due diligence and post-acquisition growth, as white-labelled services
("Search as a Service", "Entrepreneurship as a Service") at a fraction of what traditional providers charge. Its
SaaS and MaaS (M&A as a Service) models carry the same idea into the companies it supports: practical tools, AI
first among them, that improve decisions and operations in businesses that have never had them.

In one line: help great operators buy good businesses and turn them into great ones, using technology and an
operator's playbook instead of leverage alone.

## Why do you want to work at Caprae Capital?

Because the work is real and the feedback loop is short. SaaSquatch is a good example: an internal sourcing tool
built in-house, used daily by the team and sold to customers (1,200+ users and 30+ paying customers in its first
three weeks, per the handbook's references). I want to build software that someone uses the next morning to make a
decision about a real business, not features that wait in a backlog for a quarter.

I also share the operator-first view of value creation. Building LeadLens for this handbook made it concrete for me:
the hard part of sourcing isn't finding companies, it's deciding which owner to call first and what to say. That is
an engineering problem (data quality, verification, enrichment, ranking) *and* a business one. Caprae is one of the
few places where a full-stack developer sits that close to both.

## How is Caprae changing the ETA space and broader PE?

**It productises the search.** Traditional search funds expect each searcher to rebuild sourcing, outreach and
diligence from scratch, often spending one to two years and most of their capital just finding a deal. Caprae turns
those steps into services and software that searchers, independent sponsors and PE firms can plug into, reporting
10–20 owner engagements a month from direct outreach, at 30–60% less than traditional providers. That lowers the
cost of entry and lets more operators buy businesses.

**It moves the value to after the close.** By treating AI-readiness, tech development, branding and operational
support as part of the deal, not an afterthought, Caprae pushes PE away from "buy, lever, wait" toward real
business transformation. That is the difference between owning a company and improving one, and it is how lower
middle-market businesses compete with larger rivals.

**It challenges the industry with data and transparency.** Caprae has published investor rankings, research on
search-fund CEO termination rates, and a re-examination of Stanford GSB's ETA studies across demographics. It has
also formed an MBA Search Fund Alliance with student clubs. Questioning an asset class's accepted numbers in public
is unusual in private equity, and it builds the trust that both sellers and searchers need.
